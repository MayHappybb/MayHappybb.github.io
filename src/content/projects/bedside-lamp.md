I wanted a lamp that does a few things well: switch it with my voice, no cable, high colour rendering, and no flicker. And I wanted to take one product all the way from zero to the finished thing.

The requirements, as written down:

- 32 LEDs in two colour temperatures, 2200 K and 5000 K, with CRI 97 and R9 90.
- From 0.1 lumen moonlight to about 400 lumen.
- DC dimming with current sinks, so no PWM flicker at all.
- One 21700 cell lasts at least 30 days at 40 lumen for an hour a night; USB-C charging.
- An ESP32-C6 with Matter over Thread, so Apple Home, Siri and Home Assistant all work.
- Hardware protection that holds even if one part fails. A shorted MOSFET was simulated at about 2.1 A and is cut by a high-side switch.

The schematic is generated by Python from per-sheet pin-to-net maps, and the script fails if KiCad's own netlist disagrees. ngspice testbenches cover the driver loop, the filter, a reversed cell and the FET short. A Codex review of the schematic found two real bugs.

The board has 231 parts placed on about 104 × 26 mm. Nothing is routed or ordered yet.
