I wanted to explain my field without slides. The brief to the agents was short: "never make it look like slides … like the 3blue1brown style". One continuous picture per chapter, where each new idea grows out of something already on screen.

The films are code: an HTML canvas engine with a timeline, morphing TeX formulas and a camera, one script per chapter, local neural text-to-speech for the voice, generated background music, and a renderer that turns it all into 1080p video at 60 frames a second.

- **Seeing vibrations with radar**, 26:38, eight chapters, above. It starts from things that shake too little to see (a bridge, a motor, a sleeping person), builds up phase, the FMCW chirp, the I/Q arc, MIMO and the radar data cube, then points the whole pipeline at a stay cable, a failing bearing and someone's breathing, and ends with the method's limits. My reaction when it finished: "this is absolutely amazing!"
- **Invisible Motion**, 5:55, a shorter first take on the same story, made with Codex. [Watch it (6.8 MB)](/media/invisible-motion.mp4).
- **How the Fourier transform works**, 1:49. A test of the explainer skill: DeepSeek made it unattended and wrote an honest report of every problem it hit with the skill.
