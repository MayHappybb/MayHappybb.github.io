---
title: "10 µm copper foil test: it worked"
date: 2023-11-17
summary: "A folder name from November 2023, and how a sheet of kitchen-thin metal became a paper about hearing sound from 30 metres away."
icon: ear
---

A millimetre-wave radar can "hear" sound: sound makes a surface vibrate by micrometres, and the radar reads that motion from the phase of the echo. The catch is the surface. A paper cup, a snack bag or a metal plate all respond differently, and many respond badly. Range was a few metres at best.

In autumn 2023 I started folding very thin films into corner reflectors. A corner reflector sends the radar's wave straight back, so even a small one is bright. A thin film moves with the air. Put them together and you get something that is easy for the radar to see and easy for sound to shake, whatever the real source is made of.

From November 2023 to January 2024 I tried copper foil of 0.5, 6 and 10 µm, 20 µm aluminium and 25 µm PET, with and without a cavity behind, in several sizes, playing "To be or not to be" through a speaker at 90 to 108 dB and recovering the audio from the radar. The folder for 17 November is named 「10um铜箔测试_成了」: the 10 µm copper foil test, it worked. A later note on the 0.5 µm foil just says 「非常非常清楚」, very, very clear.

In May 2024 came the long-range runs: distances from 4.3 to 30 m, voice, tones and frequency sweeps. At 30 m it still worked. That became the first paper of my PhD: a passive relay that makes radar sound sensing independent of the source, at more than 7.5 times the range of earlier methods ([IEEE IoT-J, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/JIOT.2024.3525079)).

The first draft had a placeholder for the distance, in red: "15m". It ended up twice that.
